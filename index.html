STACK: Student Therapy and Animal Care Kit

Foster for a month · a week · a day · a few hours

a · Our story

Two students who missed their pets.

Student life can get stressful, especially without our pets from home to help us unwind. Adopting doesn't make sense in a college apartment, but what about fostering? That's how the idea behind STACK was born.

Here's how we help. Our kit gives students an easy first step: cards that explain why fostering matters, how to apply and what approval looks like, plus "Meet" cards for real animals waiting at Boston-area shelters and a fidget-toy game to keep their hands busy. For shelters, that means more students who foster, volunteer and donate, and more animals who get a break from the kennel. That break helps them on their way to a forever home.

b · The benefit

Good for the animal. Good for you.

Short-term fostering helps on both ends of the leash.

For animals

14×

Dogs who are fostered are over 14× more likely to be adopted.

A night or two in a home lowers a shelter animal's stress. It also shows the shelter how that animal behaves around new people and places. That gives future adopters more to go on, and proof the animal can settle into a home.

For students

10 min

Studies show that just 10 minutes with a cat or dog lowers cortisol, your body's main stress hormone.

Lots of students want a pet but can't adopt while they're only at college for a few years. Fostering lets you have one for as long as you can.

c · How to foster

Interested?

Every shelter has an application, so plan ahead. Most cover food, supplies and vet care, so fostering costs you very little. Ask each shelter which lengths they offer.

A month

Take an animal home and help it settle before adoption.

A week

Cover a busy stretch at the shelter or a gap between placements.

A day

A day out for a dog or a quiet day in for a cat.

A few hours

Walks, play and cuddles between classes.

d · Approval process

The general rules

Each shelter has its own process, but this is generally what to expect. It takes a little time, but it's worth it in the end!

  1. Apply

    Share your home, roommates, pet experience and the animal you'd like to foster.

  2. Train

    Attend a short orientation on care basics and shelter rules.

  3. Match

    The shelter pairs you with an animal that fits your lifestyle.

  4. Agree

    Sign a foster contract with care steps and emergency contacts.

Some shelters are more thorough, with a virtual home visit and reference checks. They'll share everything they know about your animal and teach you how to introduce it to other pets, neighbors and roommates.

Other ways to help

One of the biggest struggles shelters face is finding enough volunteers and fosters. If you can't bring an animal home, you can still help. Pick a shelter to see how.

Be the first friendly face a rescue dog meets. Gals Best Pal brings rescue dogs up to Massachusetts, and volunteers help out during their 48-hour arrival quarantine.

Anytime · Anywhere

Show up and spread the word

Go to an adoption event. Shelters hold them in dog-friendly places like breweries and independent pet stores, and lots of animals get adopted there, sometimes by people who only came to support. Follow shelters on Instagram to hear about events first, and contact any shelter to learn about more opportunities.
